Find surfaces with a camera

          
The projector shows a sequence of stripe patterns while a camera standing somewhere near it photographs each one. Decoding the photos tells which projector pixel lit each camera pixel. A flat surface shows up as a region where that mapping is a single perspective transform. The camera's exact position doesn't matter, but it must be off to one side: the parallax is the signal.

Upgrade to a 3D model

          
Treats each group of welded surfaces as a box standing on the floor, then solves for the projector's aim and lens and each box's size and position from the corners. One viewpoint can't tell absolute size, so the solve needs two tape measurements. The lens offset comes from the projector's spec sheet: 0% means the image is centred on the lens, 100% means it sits entirely above it. Aiming lower and shifting the image up look almost the same in the corners, so the solve can't work this out on its own. The throw ratio (distance divided by image width, such as 1.20 : 1) is optional but helps a lot: from one viewpoint, a wider lens aimed flatter can fit the corners almost as well as the real one. This simulated projector's is 0.87. After a camera scan, the line where the wall meets the floor is used too.
